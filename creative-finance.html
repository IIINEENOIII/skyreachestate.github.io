Creative finance — what it is, and when it actually helps.

Sometimes a traditional cash offer isn't your best outcome. In certain situations, alternative structures can produce a cleaner result — especially when timeline or equity is tight. Here's what each one means, in plain English.

Important: not every property or seller qualifies for these structures, and they carry real trade-offs. We'll explain the risks honestly and only propose an option that genuinely fits your situation — if a simple cash sale is better for you, we'll say that instead.
Structure 01

Seller financing

You act as the bank: the buyer pays you over time instead of all at once. Can make sense if you'd prefer monthly income over a lump sum, or when the property's condition makes traditional financing difficult. Terms — down payment, rate, length — are negotiated upfront.

Structure 02

Subject-to (existing mortgage)

We purchase the home and take over your monthly payments while your existing mortgage stays in place — title transfers to us. This can stop the clock fast when payments are behind, but it has real risks we'll walk through before anything is signed.

Structure 03

Wrap / wraparound

A new agreement "wraps" around the existing loan with one payment stream. Occasionally useful when the current rate or terms make a creative structure attractive. Must be documented carefully — and isn't available in every scenario.

Structure 04

Lease option

A buyer leases now with the option to buy later. Sometimes fits when a seller wants time or a higher price down the road. Not ideal for distressed or urgent situations — and it requires airtight written terms.

Structure 05

Novation-style sale

A structured agreement that can maximize your sale price when the home needs work you can't manage yourself. Not instant — the timeline depends on the market — but worth discussing when price matters more than speed.

Honest answer

When cash is simply best

Foreclosure deadlines, vacant homes at risk of vandalism, major repairs, estates that need closure — sometimes the cleanest outcome is a fast, simple, as-is cash sale. We'll never dress up a creative structure to make it sound better than it is.

What sellers usually ask

The four questions that actually matter.

How fast can it close?

Cash is fastest. Creative structures take longer because the paperwork has to be right — rushing documentation is how people get hurt.

Will it fall apart?

We'll tell you exactly what has to go right for each option — and what happens if it doesn't. No structure we propose should depend on luck.

What do I walk away with?

Net proceeds, monthly cash flow, or debt relief — every option gets compared on the same sheet so you're choosing with real numbers.

What are the risks to me?

Every structure has them, including the "simple" ones. We put them in writing and encourage you to have your own attorney review anything before you sign.

Which structure fits your situation?

Tell us your timeline and what's pressuring you.

Payments, repairs, liens, a sale date — lay it out in your own words and we'll tell you which options are actually realistic.
